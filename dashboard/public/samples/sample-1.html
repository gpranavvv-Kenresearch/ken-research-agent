New Zealand Mobility Aid Market to Reach USD 185M by 2031

By Ken Research

According to Ken Research, New Zealand's mobility-aid market covers mobility products and associated services supplied through public equipment programmes, injury rehabilitation, institutional procurement and private retail. The market is estimated at USD 122 million in 2025 and forecast to reach USD 185 million by 2031, representing a 7.19% CAGR during 2026–2031. The New Zealand Mobility Aid Market report provides the underlying scope, segmentation and forecast evidence.

Demand is expanding as population ageing, longer community-based living and recurring replacement needs increase the installed equipment base. The commercial thesis is more specific than simple volume growth: value is moving toward powered wheelchairs, mobility scooters, customised seating and lifecycle services. Import exposure, controlled public procurement and nationwide repair obligations are the counterweight, making service capability and landed-cost discipline as important as product breadth. For decision-makers, the result signals a widening but operationally demanding revenue pool.

Market Definition and Evidence Snapshot

The market includes manual and powered wheelchairs, mobility scooters, walking aids, transfer equipment, configuration services and related replacement parts; it does not represent the wider value of disability care. Growth Mechanisms and Market Economics

Market growth comes from a larger user base, periodic replacement and a richer equipment mix rather than one demand event. Ageing supports unit demand, while powered controls, modular seating, batteries and configuration raise value per user. Public funding establishes a demand floor, but private purchases and service revenue determine how much growth converts into resilient supplier margins.

What is expanding the demand base?

Stats NZ's 2025 projections show that the 65-plus population reached 874,000 in 2024 and is likely to reach one million by 2029. This expands demand across rollators, wheelchairs, scooters and transfer aids. Replacement cycles, battery renewal and reassessment amplify the effect because users' mobility and postural needs change over time.

Which funding and technology mechanisms matter most?

A New Zealand Ministry of Health briefing states that equipment support reaches around 100,000 disabled people of all ages. Assessment and approved-supplier pathways therefore create recurring demand, while the New Zealand e-health market provides adjacent context for remote diagnostics and connected monitoring. The payoff depends on demonstrable clinical value, repairability and data safeguards.

Where Market Value Is Moving

Value is moving from standard hardware toward powered, configured and service-intensive solutions. Ken Research expects market value to grow faster than unit volume because electronic systems and custom components carry higher prices. Buyers are also placing greater weight on fitting, uptime and repair response, which shifts revenue from one-off transactions toward equipment-lifecycle relationships.

Which product mix creates the largest value pool?

Within the product-type dimension, powered wheelchairs and mobility scooters generate the largest value pool; walking aids remain the largest category by units. Buyers pay for motors, batteries, controls, seating and configuration, not simply a frame. This supports after-sales revenue, but it also increases working capital, spare-parts and technician requirements.

Which segment is growing fastest?

Technology is the fastest-growing dimension, led by electrically powered, smart-connected, sensor-enabled and custom-modular equipment. The related New Zealand memory-care market illustrates the wider need for safe, supported mobility in ageing-care settings. Commercial success will favour devices that combine independence with maintainability rather than features that add complexity without measurable user benefit.

Competition, Regulation and Entry Barriers

Competition is moderately fragmented and is shaped by approved product access, clinical support, inventory availability and after-sales service. Ken Research identifies Cubro, Allied Medical, Invacare New Zealand, Medifab and Permobil New Zealand among the participants; because public share values are not displayed, these companies should be treated as an unranked competitive set.

What determines competitive advantage?

National repair coverage and configuration capability are harder to reproduce than a product catalogue. Suppliers must support dispersed users, carry critical parts and manage loan or replacement equipment. The Australia mobility-aid market offers a relevant regional benchmark for funded access and home-care channels, although New Zealand procurement and service economics require separate modelling.

What regulation and risk shape entry?

For products regulated as medical devices, Medsafe's WAND requirements state that devices must be notified within 30 calendar days of a person or organisation becoming the sponsor. Traceability and post-market responsibilities create a practical entry barrier. Ken Research also estimates 82% import dependence, exposing suppliers to freight, currency and delayed contract-price recovery.

Decision Framework and Market Outlook

The base case is steady expansion to USD 185 million by 2031, with value growth outpacing unit growth. Faster assessment pathways and greater private adoption of powered mobility would strengthen this direction. Currency pressure, procurement restraint or poor national service capacity would weaken it by delaying purchases or compressing supplier margins.

Decision Framework

Executives should translate the forecast into three operating choices:

  • Suppliers: tier the portfolio across standard, powered and complex products while pricing maintenance and battery replacement explicitly.
  • Funders: evaluate total lifecycle cost, repair turnaround and reissue potential alongside the initial equipment price.
  • Entrants and investors: secure compliant sponsorship, regional service partners and foreign-exchange protection before scaling inventory.

Signals to Monitor

Track funded-equipment recipient volumes, assessment waiting times, public demand share, unit growth, blended selling prices, exchange rates and repair turnaround. Also monitor private-payment capacity through the adjacent New Zealand long-term-care private insurance market. Together, these indicators reveal whether forecast growth reflects wider access, premium mix or price inflation.

What does the New Zealand mobility-aid market include?

It includes supplier revenue from manual and powered wheelchairs, mobility scooters, walking aids, transfer equipment, configuration services and associated replacement parts. Demand flows through publicly funded equipment programmes, injury rehabilitation, institutional procurement and private retail. The definition does not include the full value of disability care, residential care or unrelated medical equipment.

How large was the market in 2025?

Ken Research estimates the New Zealand mobility-aid market at USD 122 million in 2025. This is a market estimate, not an official national-accounts statistic. The report associates the value with approximately 110,000 units supplied across public and private pathways, while recognising that product prices vary substantially by configuration and technology.

What is the forecast value and growth rate?

Ken Research forecasts the market to reach USD 185 million in 2031, representing a 7.19% CAGR during 2026–2031. The projection assumes continued population ageing, replacement demand, community-based living and a richer mix of powered and configured products. It remains sensitive to funding decisions, import costs and supplier service capacity.

Which segment and competitive factors matter most?

Powered wheelchairs and mobility scooters form the largest product value pool, while technology is the fastest-growing segmentation dimension. Competition centres on approved ranges, clinical fitting, inventory, regional repairs and after-sales support. What is the primary opportunity or risk?

The strongest opportunity is to combine higher-value powered or modular equipment with fitting, maintenance, batteries, spare parts and refurbishment. The main risk is the interaction between import dependence and public procurement controls: currency or freight increases can raise landed costs before contract prices adjust, while inadequate repair coverage can undermine service performance.
